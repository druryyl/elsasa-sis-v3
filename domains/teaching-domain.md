# Teaching Domain

## Purpose

The Teaching domain manages the people, qualifications, availability, capacity, and assignments required to perform teaching work.

It is the authoritative source for:

* who is recognized by the school as a teacher or learning facilitator
* what teaching capabilities and qualifications the person has
* what teaching work the person is authorized to perform
* when the person is available for teaching work
* what teaching responsibility has been assigned to the person
* how much teaching capacity has been committed or remains available
* how substitutions, reassignments, and assignment exceptions are handled
* the history of teaching authorizations and assignments

The domain begins when a person is proposed as a teaching resource and continues while the person's teaching profile, authorization, availability, capacity, assignments, and required history must be maintained.

Teaching does not define what a student must learn, record what happened during learning, schedule the activity into a time and room, or manage employment and payroll.

---

## Domain Responsibility

The Teaching domain owns:

* teaching profile
* teaching capability
* qualification evidence and verification status for teaching purposes
* teaching authorization and restrictions
* teacher availability for assignment
* teaching capacity
* teaching demand intake
* teacher eligibility for requested work
* teaching assignment
* assignment role and responsibility
* workload commitment and workload exception
* substitution and reassignment
* teaching assignment completion
* teaching history
* correction of approved Teaching records

Teaching is the authoritative source of teacher identity in a teaching capacity, teacher eligibility, availability, assignment, and workload.

Other domains may provide employee identity, student context, academic demand, development-support demand, calendar, schedule, room, or approval context. They must not independently create or change a Teaching Assignment.

Teaching authorizes who performs specified teaching work. It does not own the academic work performed, the operational timetable, employee administration, or compensation.

---

## Scope

### In Scope

#### Teaching Profile

Manage a person's recognized relationship with the school as a teaching resource.

A Teaching Profile may include:

* person or employee identity reference
* professional or display name
* teaching role
* campus, department, or organizational affiliation
* teaching capabilities
* active, restricted, suspended, or inactive teaching standing
* effective dates
* source and approving authority

A Teaching Profile is not a complete employee record.

Until an employee or Human Resources domain is defined, Teaching may hold the minimum identity information required to identify and assign a teacher. It must not become the owner of employment contracts, payroll, benefits, or general personnel administration merely because that information concerns a teacher.

The domain must support teaching resources who may not be conventional employees when school policy permits them, such as:

* contract teachers
* visiting teachers
* external assessors
* volunteers
* specialist instructors
* learning facilitators

Whether these categories are permitted and what they may do are policy questions.

---

#### Teaching Capability

Manage the kinds of teaching work a person may potentially perform.

Capabilities may relate to:

* subject or learning area
* level or stage
* program
* course, module, project, or other learning model
* delivery mode
* language of instruction
* specialist activity
* assessment, moderation, or supervision responsibility

A capability describes potential suitability. It does not by itself authorize a teacher or create an assignment.

The capability structure must remain curriculum independent. Teaching must not assume every school organizes learning by class and subject.

---

#### Qualification Evidence

Manage evidence used to support a person's teaching capability or authorization.

Qualification evidence may include:

* qualification or certification type
* issuing authority
* issue date
* expiry date, when applicable
* evidence reference
* claimed, submitted, verified, rejected, expired, or revoked state
* verification actor and date
* restrictions or conditions

The system must distinguish a person's claim, submitted evidence, verified evidence, and the school's authorization decision.

A verified qualification does not automatically create a Teaching Authorization or Teaching Assignment.

---

#### Teaching Authorization

Manage the school's permission for a teaching resource to perform specified teaching work.

An authorization may define:

* authorized capability or role
* applicable campus, program, level, or delivery mode
* effective period
* required supervision
* restrictions or conditions
* decision authority
* supporting qualification evidence
* active, restricted, suspended, expired, or revoked state

Authorization must be explicit when school policy requires it.

An authorization establishes eligibility. It does not reserve capacity or assign work.

---

#### Teacher Availability

Manage when and under what conditions a teacher may be considered for teaching work.

Availability may include:

* effective date range
* available days or periods
* campus availability
* delivery-mode availability
* temporary unavailability
* recurring restrictions
* maximum available teaching capacity
* reason and source

Availability is not an operational timetable.

Teaching states whether capacity may be assigned. Operations decides when and where an assigned activity is operationally scheduled.

Employment leave, working hours, or contractual limits may eventually be owned by an employee or Human Resources domain. Teaching consumes their effect on teaching availability without redefining the source decision.

---

#### Teaching Demand

Receive and interpret a request for teaching capacity from the domain that owns the underlying work.

An Academic teaching demand may include:

* learning unit or activity reference
* student or learning-group context
* academic period
* required teaching capability
* required assignment role
* expected effort or workload basis
* required assessment authorization
* campus or delivery constraints
* requested effective period
* number of teaching resources required

A Development demand may request a teacher or facilitator for an authorized non-academic responsibility. Such participation must not automatically be treated as academic teaching.

Teaching owns staffing the demand. It does not redefine the purpose, content, participants, or outcome of the underlying Academic or Development work.

---

#### Teacher Eligibility

Determine whether a teacher may be considered for specified teaching work.

Eligibility may consider:

* active Teaching Profile
* required Teaching Authorization
* verified qualification, when required
* effective dates
* availability
* current workload and remaining capacity
* campus or delivery restriction
* required supervision
* unresolved conflicts

Eligibility criteria and exception authority must come from approved school policy.

Eligibility does not guarantee assignment. It is one input to an authorized assignment decision.

---

#### Teaching Assignment

Manage the authorization that connects a teacher to defined teaching work.

A Teaching Assignment should identify:

* assigned teacher
* source demand or work reference
* assignment role
* scope of responsibility
* student, learning group, or learning context when applicable
* effective start and end
* expected workload commitment
* relevant authorization and qualification checks
* assignment authority
* assignment state
* predecessor, replacement, or related assignment when applicable

Possible assignment roles may include primary teacher, co-teacher, assistant, facilitator, assessor, moderator, supervisor, or substitute. These examples are not mandatory universal categories.

The school's approved roles and their authority must be explicit.

Teaching Assignment is distinct from an academic Assignment issued to a student. The qualified term **Teaching Assignment** must be used whenever ambiguity is possible.

---

#### Workload and Capacity

Manage how Teaching Assignments consume teacher capacity.

Workload may include:

* planned workload
* committed workload
* remaining capacity
* direct teaching effort
* assessment or moderation responsibility
* approved non-contact effort
* overload or under-allocation
* approved exception
* workload by effective period

The school determines which activities count, the unit of measurement, calculation rules, limits, and approval authority.

Agents must not assume workload is always measured in classroom hours.

Academics may provide activity completion and academic-effort context. Teaching determines the effect on teaching workload under approved policy.

---

#### Capacity and Assignment Conflict

Identify conditions that may prevent or question an assignment.

Examples include:

* missing or expired authorization
* unverified required qualification
* teacher unavailable during the required period
* workload limit exceeded
* overlapping commitments
* incompatible campus or delivery requirement
* inactive Teaching Profile
* missing supervision
* unresolved operational schedule conflict

A conflict, warning, exception, and rejection are different business meanings.

The system must not automatically reject or approve an exception unless explicit policy authorizes that result.

---

#### Substitution and Reassignment

Manage temporary or permanent changes to an existing Teaching Assignment.

This may include:

* request for coverage
* selection of an eligible replacement
* temporary substitution
* permanent reassignment
* partial transfer of responsibility
* assignment suspension
* assignment cancellation
* handover requirement
* effective period
* reason and approval

The original assignment and all changes must remain historically interpretable.

A scheduling change does not automatically change a Teaching Assignment. An assignment change does not automatically alter academic records already produced by the previous authorized teacher.

---

#### Assignment Completion

Record that an assigned teaching responsibility has reached its authorized end or completion condition.

Completion may consider:

* end of effective assignment period
* completion information from Academics or Development
* unresolved responsibilities
* required handover
* approval or confirmation

Teaching records completion of the responsibility. Academics owns whether learning, assessment, or academic requirements were completed.

Completion must not erase the assignment or its workload history.

---

#### Teaching History

Preserve the history of a person's teaching relationship and responsibilities.

Examples include:

* profile established or changed
* qualification submitted, verified, expired, rejected, or revoked
* authorization granted, restricted, suspended, expired, or revoked
* availability changed
* assignment proposed, approved, changed, completed, or cancelled
* substitution or reassignment
* workload exception approved
* record corrected

Teaching history answers what the person was authorized and assigned to do. It does not duplicate lesson activity, attendance, assessment, student results, or employee-performance records.

---

## Out of Scope

### Students

Teaching does not own:

* student identity
* parent or guardian identity and authority
* admission or enrollment
* student administrative placement
* student status
* leave, transfer, withdrawal, promotion, or graduation

### Academics

Teaching does not own:

* curriculum or learning structure
* student learning plan or academic participation
* lesson content or learning activity record
* academic attendance
* student assignments and submissions
* assessment evidence
* grades, results, or feedback
* learning progress
* academic intervention
* report card or transcript
* academic standing, promotion recommendation, or graduation eligibility

### Development

Teaching does not own:

* behaviour observation or discipline
* counselling or wellbeing
* non-academic intervention
* extracurricular program purpose
* extracurricular participation or attendance
* non-academic achievement
* confidential Development records

### Finance

Teaching does not own:

* billing or payment
* scholarship financial decision
* discount, waiver, or refund
* financial clearance
* teacher compensation or payroll

### Operations

Teaching does not own:

* academic calendar
* timetable
* room allocation
* school event
* announcement
* operational schedule
* general operational approval

### Other Exclusions

The Teaching domain does not own:

* employee recruitment and onboarding
* employment contracts
* employee leave decisions
* payroll, benefits, tax, or compensation
* general personnel records
* employee discipline or grievance
* occupational health records
* formal employee performance appraisal
* learning content authoring
* video learning or LMS content delivery
* generic identity and access management

Some of these concepts may affect teaching eligibility or availability. Teaching should consume only the authorized outcome needed for its workflow.

## Actors

The exact actor names and authority levels may differ between schools.

### Teacher or Learning Facilitator

A person recognized as a teaching resource.

May:

* provide required teaching-profile information
* submit qualification evidence
* declare availability when policy permits
* view proposed and approved assignments
* accept or acknowledge an assignment when required
* request an assignment change or substitution
* report a capacity or eligibility conflict
* confirm handover or completion when authorized

A teacher does not assign themselves merely by performing or recording academic work.

### Teaching Coordinator

Coordinates teaching capacity and assignments.

May:

* review teaching demand
* search for eligible teaching resources
* propose assignments
* identify capacity conflicts
* arrange substitution or reassignment
* monitor assignment and workload exceptions

Approval authority must be defined by school policy.

### Department, Subject, or Program Coordinator

Provides domain-specific capability and suitability context.

May:

* clarify required teaching capability
* review subject or program suitability
* recommend a teacher
* participate in assignment or authorization review

A recommendation is not an approved Teaching Assignment unless the actor has explicit assignment authority.

### Qualification Verifier

Reviews qualification evidence for teaching purposes.

May:

* verify source and validity
* record verification outcome
* identify expiry or restriction
* request additional evidence

Verification does not itself grant a Teaching Authorization unless policy combines those authorities.

### Academic Coordinator or Academic Authority

Represents academic demand and requirements.

May:

* request teaching capacity
* specify academic capability and responsibility requirements
* clarify student or learning context
* report completion or changed academic demand
* receive the approved assignment

Academics does not independently change the Teaching Assignment.

### Operations Scheduler

Creates and coordinates the operational timetable after receiving assignment and activity context.

May:

* consume teacher availability and assignment
* identify schedule conflicts
* request assignment or availability review
* publish or change operational schedule information

The scheduler does not redefine teacher qualification, authorization, workload, or assignment.

### School Leadership or Teaching Authority

Provides authorization and decisions within approved policy.

May:

* approve Teaching Profiles or authorizations
* approve assignments
* approve workload exceptions
* resolve escalated conflicts
* suspend or revoke teaching authorization
* approve corrections

### Employee or Human Resources Authority

When such a function exists, provides authoritative employment context such as:

* employee identity
* employment standing
* contractual working limits
* approved leave or separation

Teaching consumes the minimum necessary outcome. It does not copy the complete personnel record.

### System Administrator

May configure access or technical operation.

A System Administrator does not gain business authority to verify qualifications, authorize teaching, assign teachers, or approve workload exceptions merely through technical access.

## Core Business Concepts

### Teaching Profile

The school's recognized representation of a person as a teaching resource.

### Teaching Capability

A category of teaching work the person may potentially be suitable to perform.

### Qualification Evidence

Evidence supporting a claimed Teaching Capability or authorization decision.

### Teaching Authorization

An effective school decision permitting a teacher to perform defined teaching work under stated conditions.

### Teacher Availability

The effective periods and conditions under which a teacher may be considered for assignment.

### Teaching Demand

A request from an owning domain for qualified teaching capacity to perform defined work.

### Teacher Eligibility

The result of evaluating a teacher against the requirements for particular work.

### Teaching Assignment

The authorized connection between a teacher and defined teaching responsibility.

### Assignment Role

The teacher's authorized responsibility within a Teaching Assignment.

### Workload Commitment

The teaching capacity reserved or consumed by an assignment under approved policy.

### Capacity Conflict

A condition requiring resolution before or during an assignment.

### Workload Exception

An authorized departure from a workload rule or limit.

### Substitution

A temporary assignment of another eligible person to cover an existing responsibility.

### Reassignment

A permanent or materially changed allocation of teaching responsibility.

### Teaching History

The preserved record of teaching profile, qualification, authorization, availability, assignment, and workload decisions over time.

### Teaching Record Correction

An authorized, auditable correction that preserves the original Teaching record and the reason for change.

        │
        ▼
Teaching Profile Established
        │
        ▼
Capability and Qualification Reviewed
        │
        ▼
Teaching Authorization Granted
        │
        ▼

        │
        ▼
Teaching Demand Received
        │
        ▼
Eligibility and Capacity Evaluated
        │
        ▼
Teaching Assignment Approved
        │
        ├───────────────┐
        ▼               ▼
Work Performed     Conflict or Absence
        │               │
        │               ▼

## Main Workflows

## 1. Establish or Change Teaching Profile

### Trigger

A person is proposed as a teaching resource, or an existing Teaching Profile requires an authorized change.

### Actor

* Teaching Coordinator
* Teaching Authority
* Teacher or Learning Facilitator
* Employee or Human Resources Authority, when applicable

### Input

* person or employee identity reference
* proposed teaching relationship
* professional information required by policy
* organizational affiliation
* effective date
* supporting authority

### Outcome

* Teaching Profile is established, changed, restricted, or made inactive
* effective history is preserved
* affected authorizations and assignments can be identified

### Business Rules

* a Teaching Profile must identify one teaching resource unambiguously
* establishment must not imply employment or payroll eligibility
* inactive or restricted standing must not silently erase existing history
* changes affecting active assignments require review

---

## 2. Submit and Verify Qualification Evidence

### Trigger

A qualification is required, submitted, renewed, disputed, expired, or found inaccurate.

### Actor

* Teacher or Learning Facilitator
* Qualification Verifier
* Teaching Authority

### Input

* qualification claim
* evidence and issuing source
* issue and expiry dates
* capability or authorization purpose
* verification criteria

### Outcome

* evidence is recorded with its verification state
* restrictions, expiry, or rejection are explicit
* affected authorizations and assignments can be reviewed

### Business Rules

* claim, evidence, verification, and authorization are distinct
* verification must identify actor, date, source, and outcome; verification must be performed by a Qualification Verifier or school-designated authority; verification confirms authenticity and acceptance of a qualification; verification alone does not grant Teaching Authorization
* recognized qualification issuers are configurable school policy; the system must not hard-code recognized issuers
* renewal requirements are defined per Qualification Type; some qualifications expire and require renewal or re-verification; others remain permanently valid
* expired or revoked evidence must remain historical
* agents must not infer equivalence between qualifications

---

## 3. Grant, Restrict, Suspend, or Revoke Teaching Authorization

### Trigger

A teaching resource requires an authorization decision or an existing authorization requires review.

### Actor

* Teaching Authority
* Qualification Verifier
* School Leadership

### Input

* Teaching Profile
* capability requirements
* verified qualification context
* applicable policy
* proposed scope, conditions, and effective period

### Outcome

* authorization is granted, restricted, suspended, revoked, rejected, or allowed to expire
* decision basis and authority are preserved
* affected assignments can be identified

### Business Rules

* authorization is granted by Teaching Authority following workflow: Qualification Verified → Teaching Coordinator Review → Teaching Authority Approval → Teaching Authorization Granted
* authorization may consider verified qualifications, capability, suitability, and school policy requirements; qualification verification alone does not automatically grant authorization
* supported authorization states: Active, Restricted, Suspended, Revoked, Expired; conditional authorization state Authorized Under Supervision is supported; Phase-2 records supervision requirements and may record supervising teacher reference; Phase-2 does not require a complex supervision workflow
* authorization scope and effective dates must be explicit
* authorization must not automatically create an assignment
* status changes are controlled by Teaching Authority; status changes must preserve history; authorization decisions remain auditable
* suspension or revocation must trigger review of affected current and future assignments
* formal appeal workflows are out of scope for Phase-2
* adverse decisions require the authority and process defined by policy

---

## 4. Establish or Change Teacher Availability

### Trigger

Teaching capacity becomes available, changes, or becomes temporarily unavailable.

### Actor

* Teacher or Learning Facilitator
* Teaching Coordinator
* Employee or Human Resources Authority, when applicable
* Teaching Authority

### Input

* effective period
* available or unavailable capacity
* applicable campus or delivery conditions
* source and reason
* employment or operational constraint when relevant

### Outcome

* availability is established or changed
* conflicts with active or proposed assignments are identified
* Operations can consume the authorized constraint

### Business Rules

* availability and operational schedule are distinct
* source-owned employment decisions must not be re-decided by Teaching
* retroactive changes require explicit authority and impact review
* unavailability must not silently cancel an assignment

---

## 5. Receive and Clarify Teaching Demand

### Trigger

Academics, Development, or another authorized owner requests teaching capacity.

### Actor

* Academic Coordinator or Academic Authority
* Development Coordinator, when applicable
* Teaching Coordinator

### Input

* source-domain work reference
* required capability and role
* participant or learning context
* effective period
* expected workload basis
* qualification, authorization, campus, or delivery constraints

### Outcome

* demand is accepted for staffing, returned for clarification, changed by its owner, cancelled, or declined with reason
* ownership of the underlying work remains explicit

### Business Rules

* Teaching must not invent missing academic or Development requirements
* duplicate demand must be identified without erasing either source request
* demand does not reserve teacher capacity until policy says it does
* the source domain owns cancellation or material redefinition of its work

---

## 6. Evaluate Teacher Eligibility and Capacity

### Trigger

A teacher is considered for defined Teaching Demand.

### Actor

* Teaching Coordinator
* Department, Subject, or Program Coordinator
* Teaching Authority

### Input

* Teaching Demand
* Teaching Profile
* capability and authorization
* qualification context
* availability
* existing workload commitments
* relevant operational constraints

### Outcome

* eligible candidates and unresolved conflicts are identified
* evaluation basis is explainable
* required exception or approval is identified

### Business Rules

* eligibility rules must come from policy
* suitability recommendation and eligibility decision are distinct
* lack of recorded information must not be treated as confirmed eligibility
* eligibility evaluation must use information effective for the assignment period

---

## 7. Propose and Approve Teaching Assignment

### Trigger

Teaching Demand is ready to be staffed with an eligible teaching resource.

### Actor

* Teaching Coordinator
* Teaching Authority
* Teacher or Learning Facilitator, when acknowledgement is required

### Input

* Teaching Demand
* proposed teacher and assignment role
* eligibility result
* effective period
* expected workload commitment
* conflicts or exception approvals

### Outcome

* assignment is proposed, approved, rejected, withdrawn, or returned for revision
* capacity is committed when approval becomes effective
* Academics or the requesting domain receives the authorized assignment
* Operations receives the information required for scheduling

### Business Rules

* assignment authority must be explicit
* assignment must define scope, role, and effective dates
* teacher acknowledgement is not the same as assignment approval
* approval must not create academic activity or an operational timetable
* assignment history must be preserved

---

## 8. Change, Suspend, or Cancel Teaching Assignment

### Trigger

The responsibility, effective period, authorization, demand, or ability to perform an assignment changes.

### Actor

* Teaching Coordinator
* Teaching Authority
* Teacher or Learning Facilitator
* Requesting-domain authority

### Input

* current assignment
* requested change
* reason and effective date
* source-domain demand change
* eligibility, availability, or conflict context
* approval

### Outcome

* assignment is amended, suspended, cancelled, or retained
* workload commitment is adjusted prospectively or by authorized correction
* affected domains are notified
* original history remains interpretable

### Business Rules

* a schedule change alone must not silently alter assignment authority
* changes must distinguish future effect from correction of past information
* work already performed remains attributed according to the authorization effective at that time
* cancellation must not erase the source demand or prior assignment

---

## 9. Arrange Substitution or Reassignment

### Trigger

An assigned teacher cannot temporarily or permanently perform all or part of the responsibility.

### Actor

* Teacher or Learning Facilitator
* Teaching Coordinator
* Teaching Authority
* Operations Scheduler

### Input

* affected assignment
* coverage period and responsibility
* reason
* eligible substitute or replacement
* workload and schedule conflicts
* required approval

### Outcome

* substitute or replacement is assigned, coverage remains unresolved, or another authorized decision is recorded
* responsibility and effective periods remain unambiguous
* Academics and Operations receive the change

### Business Rules

* substitute eligibility must be evaluated for the covered work
* temporary substitution must not silently terminate the original assignment
* overlapping responsibility must be explicit
* retroactive substitution requires authority and must not falsify academic history

---

## 10. Review Workload and Resolve Capacity Conflict

### Trigger

An assignment creates a warning, overload, under-allocation, or other capacity conflict, or periodic workload review is due.

### Actor

* Teaching Coordinator
* Teaching Authority
* Teacher or Learning Facilitator
* School Leadership

### Input

* current and proposed assignments
* applicable workload policy
* capacity and availability
* academic-effort context
* exception request and justification

### Outcome

* conflict is resolved, assignment is changed, workload exception is approved or rejected, or escalation remains pending
* decision and impact are preserved

### Business Rules

* workload is calculated from approved Teaching Assignments; Workload Unit is configurable school policy; default unit is Teaching Hours
* Phase-2 workload management focuses on assignment capacity, workload visibility, and assignment planning; payroll calculations, compensation calculations, performance evaluation, and HR management functions are not introduced
* capacity limits: Warning (Approaching Limit — assignment remains permitted); Prohibited (Capacity Exceeded — assignment not permitted without approved exception); thresholds are policy-driven
* workload exception workflow: Teaching Coordinator recommends → Teaching Authority approves → Workload Exception Granted; teachers may not self-approve workload exceptions; exception decisions must be auditable
* workload must be derived from assignments and approved rules
* warning, policy breach, and approved exception are distinct
* an overload must not be approved merely because work has already begun
* workload figures must be reproducible for their policy version and effective period

---

## 11. Complete Teaching Assignment

### Trigger

The effective period ends or the requesting domain confirms that the assigned responsibility has concluded.

### Actor

* Teaching Coordinator
* Teacher or Learning Facilitator
* Academic or Development authority
* Teaching Authority

### Input

* assignment
* source-domain completion context
* outstanding responsibility or handover
* completion date
* required confirmation

### Outcome

* assignment is completed, ended with unresolved items, extended, or returned for review
* capacity is released according to policy
* history is preserved

### Business Rules

* completion of assignment and completion of student learning are distinct
* capacity release must use the correct effective date
* incomplete academic work remains owned by Academics
* assignment completion must not be inferred solely from timetable end when policy requires confirmation

---

## 12. Correct Approved Teaching Record

### Trigger

An approved Teaching record is found to be inaccurate or incomplete.

### Actor

* Teaching Authority
* Qualification Verifier, for authorized qualification corrections
* Teaching Coordinator
* School Leadership, when required

### Input

* original record
* requested correction
* reason and evidence
* effective date implications
* authorization

### Outcome

* corrected information becomes effective
* original information and correction history remain auditable
* affected eligibility, workload, assignments, and integrations are identified

### Business Rules

* approved records must never be silently overwritten
* correction is distinct from a new decision or changed circumstance
* retroactive correction must identify downstream impact
* academic and operational records must not be rewritten by Teaching without their owning workflows

## Business Rules

### Teaching Capacity First

Design around the real workflow of establishing, authorizing, allocating, and preserving teaching capacity.

Do not design Teaching merely as a teacher directory or timetable screen.

### Professional Profile and Employee Record Are Distinct

Teaching owns the profile required to recognize and assign a teaching resource.

Employment identity, recruitment, contract, leave, compensation, and employee appraisal belong to a future employee or Human Resources boundary unless explicitly assigned elsewhere.

### Capability, Qualification, Authorization, and Assignment Are Distinct

A person may claim a capability without evidence, hold evidence without current verification, possess a verified qualification without school authorization, and hold authorization without an active assignment.

These meanings must never be collapsed into one status.

### Demand Before Assignment

A Teaching Assignment must respond to defined work owned by Academics, Development, or another authorized domain.

Do not create assignments merely to populate workload reports.

### Assignment Authorizes Work

When a teacher is required, academic or other covered work must reference an effective Teaching Assignment.

Past work must be interpreted using the assignment and authorization effective when the work occurred.

### Availability, Assignment, and Schedule Are Distinct

Availability indicates potential capacity. Assignment authorizes responsibility. Operational scheduling determines time and place.

None should be inferred solely from another.

### Workload Is Policy-Derived

Workload is derived from authorized commitments and explicit policy.

Agents must not invent workload units, weights, limits, or overload consequences.

### Academic Work Remains Academic

Academics owns learning activities, attendance, assignments to students, assessment, results, progress, and academic completion.

Teaching may consume completion or effort context but must not duplicate the academic record.

### Effective Dating

Profile standing, qualification validity, authorization, availability, assignment, workload, substitution, and correction must retain their distinct effective dates.

The date a record is entered may differ from the date it becomes effective.

### Historical Integrity

Preserve original demands, evaluations, approvals, assignments, changes, substitutions, exceptions, completions, and corrections.

Do not overwrite history to make the current assignment appear as if it always applied.

### Recommendation Is Not Decision

A coordinator, department head, or academic authority may recommend a teacher. Only an actor with assignment authority may approve the Teaching Assignment.

### No Automatic Adverse Decision

Do not automatically suspend, revoke, reject, or cancel based solely on:

* an expired document without verifying applicability
* an unconfirmed schedule conflict
* one workload warning
* an allegation or Development record
* an academic outcome
* a financial status
* missing information that may not be required

Adverse decisions require applicable policy, authority, and appropriate process.

### Minimum Necessary Information

Teaching should consume only the identity, student, academic, Development, employment, or operational context needed to make and coordinate teaching-resource decisions.

Teaching must not copy complete student, counselling, personnel, financial, or academic records for convenience.

### No Silent Deletion

Closing, expiring, cancelling, revoking, or correcting a record must not silently erase required history.

Retention and lawful deletion remain policy questions.

## Inputs from Other Domains

### From Students

* student identity reference
* enrollment and active-status context
* administrative placement
* leave, transfer, withdrawal, promotion, and graduation events
* homeroom or administrative-group context when applicable

Teaching references this information but does not redefine it.

### From Academics

* learning demand requiring teacher assignment
* academic work reference
* required teaching capability and role
* student or learning-group context
* academic period
* expected academic effort or workload context
* assessment or moderation requirement
* demand change or cancellation
* completion of assigned academic work

Academics owns the work. Teaching owns who is assigned to perform it.

### From Development

* request for authorized teacher or facilitator participation
* required responsibility and capability
* effective period
* minimum necessary student-support guidance
* completion or cancellation of requested participation

Teaching must not consume confidential case detail merely to assign a teacher.

### From Finance

* only an authorized prerequisite relevant to a specific teaching-resource workflow

Finance does not determine teacher authorization, assignment, or workload. Payroll and teacher compensation are outside the current Finance domain.

### From Operations

* academic year and calendar context
* operational timetable
* room and campus context
* schedule conflict
* school event context
* operational approval context
* schedule change or cancellation

Teaching uses operational context but does not own the timetable or room.

### From Employee or Human Resources Capability

When such a capability exists:

* employee identity reference
* employment standing relevant to teaching eligibility
* contractual capacity constraint
* approved leave or unavailability outcome
* employment start or separation effective date

Teaching must not redefine the source employment decision.

## Outputs to Other Domains

### To Students

* authorized teacher or homeroom-role reference when required for administrative context
* effective assignment change relevant to student communication

Teaching does not change student identity, placement, or status.

### To Academics

* teacher identity reference
* approved Teaching Assignment
* assignment role and effective period
* required authorization or qualification context
* substitution, reassignment, suspension, or cancellation
* unresolved staffing status

Academics records the work performed under the assignment.

### To Development

* assigned teacher or facilitator reference
* authorized role and effective period
* substitution or assignment change
* unresolved capacity status

Development owns the non-academic purpose, participation, and outcome.

### To Finance

* only authorized teaching-role or assignment context required by a defined Finance workflow

Teaching does not calculate teacher pay or create financial obligations.

### To Operations

* approved Teaching Assignment
* teacher availability constraints
* applicable assignment role and effective dates
* scheduling requirements from the assignment
* substitution, reassignment, suspension, or cancellation
* unresolved teaching-capacity conflict

Operations schedules the activity and room without redefining the assignment.

### To Employee or Human Resources Capability

When such a capability exists:

* teaching authorization standing
* assignment and workload summary required for an authorized personnel workflow
* teaching-role change
* identified capacity conflict requiring source action

Only the minimum necessary professional context should be shared.

## Reporting Responsibility

Teaching may provide operational information such as:

* teaching resources by active standing
* qualifications awaiting verification or approaching expiry
* authorizations awaiting decision or review
* teaching demand awaiting clarification or staffing
* assignments proposed, awaiting approval, active, ending, or unresolved
* teaching demand without an assigned teacher
* available and committed teaching capacity
* workload by teacher, period, campus, program, or approved category
* overload, under-allocation, and workload exceptions
* qualification, authorization, availability, or schedule conflicts
* substitutions and reassignments requiring action
* assignment completion and handover status
* Teaching record corrections

Reports must originate from real Teaching workflows.

Do not create separate data-entry processes solely for dashboards.

Cross-domain dashboards may combine Teaching information with Students, Academics, Development, Finance, Operations, or future employee information, but ownership, effective dates, and access authority must remain explicit.

Workload reports must state the policy version, measurement basis, and period. They must not be presented as employee-performance appraisal unless an authorized business process explicitly defines that meaning.

## Guiding Principles

### Teaching Responsibility First

Design around how the school ensures that appropriate people are authorized and assigned to required work.

### Assignment Is an Authorization

A Teaching Assignment is a business authorization with scope, role, authority, and effective dates, not merely a name displayed beside a class.

### Curriculum and Learning-Model Independence

Support class-based, course-based, module-based, project-based, and individual-progress learning without making class the mandatory center of assignment.

### Separate Professional Meanings

Keep profile, capability, qualification, verification, authorization, availability, eligibility, assignment, schedule, and work performed distinct.

### Explain Capacity

Every workload or capacity result should be explainable from assignments, effective dates, and approved policy.

### Preserve Teaching History

Keep professional authorization, assignment responsibility, substitutions, exceptions, and corrections interpretable over time.

### Cross-Domain Ownership

Teaching staffs work owned by other domains. It must not absorb the academic, Development, operational, student, financial, or employee workflow merely because a teacher participates.

### Actor-Centric Workspaces

Show each actor what demand, evidence, authorization, assignment, conflict, or decision needs attention and what action they are authorized to take.

### Policy Is Explicit

Qualification rules, authorization criteria, assignment authority, workload calculation, limits, exceptions, substitution, and completion must come from approved policy.

### Simple Before Flexible

Implement the smallest capability, authorization, assignment, and workload model required by the current school. Do not create a speculative universal workforce-management system.

## Open Questions

### Teacher and Employee Boundary

* Is every teacher an employee?
* Which external, contract, volunteer, visiting, or specialist teaching resources are permitted?
* Who owns authoritative personal identity for teachers before an employee domain exists?
* Which minimum employment outcomes may Teaching consume?
* Who owns employment leave, separation, and contractual working limits?

### Teaching Profile

* Who may establish, change, restrict, or inactivate a Teaching Profile?
* Which affiliations and professional attributes are required?
* Can a person hold multiple teaching roles or campus affiliations concurrently?
* Which information may teachers maintain themselves?

### Capability, Qualification, and Authorization

* Which capabilities are recognized by the school? *(School configuration — not a blocking policy question.)*
* Which capabilities require qualification evidence? *(School configuration — not a blocking policy question.)*
* ~~Who verifies qualifications and external issuers?~~ **Resolved.** Qualification verification is performed by a Qualification Verifier or equivalent school-designated authority. Verification must be explicitly recorded. Recognized qualification issuers are configurable school policy; the system must not hard-code recognized issuers; schools maintain their own approved issuer list.
* ~~Does qualification verification expire or require renewal?~~ **Resolved.** Renewal requirements are defined per Qualification Type. Some qualifications expire and require renewal or re-verification (e.g., Teaching Certification, Child Protection Certification, Safety Certification, First Aid Certification); others remain permanently valid (e.g., Bachelor, Master, Doctoral degrees).
* ~~Who grants Teaching Authorization?~~ **Resolved.** Teaching Authorization is granted by Teaching Authority following this workflow: Qualification Verified → Teaching Coordinator Review → Teaching Authority Approval → Teaching Authorization Granted. Authorization may consider verified qualifications, capability, suitability, and school policy requirements. Qualification verification alone does not automatically grant authorization.
* ~~May authorization be conditional on supervision?~~ **Resolved.** Supported authorization states: Authorized; Authorized Under Supervision. Schools may require supervision for selected teachers. Phase-2 records supervision requirements and may record the supervising teacher reference. Phase-2 does not require a complex supervision workflow.
* ~~What process applies to restriction, suspension, revocation, and appeal?~~ **Resolved.** Teaching Authorization lifecycle states: Active, Restricted, Suspended, Revoked, Expired. Status changes are controlled by Teaching Authority; status changes must preserve history; authorization decisions remain auditable. Formal appeal workflows are out of scope for Phase-2.

### Availability and Capacity

* Who declares and approves availability?
* What is the relationship between employment working time and teaching capacity?
* How are recurring and exceptional availability represented?
* What happens to active assignments when availability changes?
* How far into the future may availability be committed?

### Teaching Demand

* Does Academics request capacity before or after learning is offered?
* What minimum information constitutes actionable demand?
* Can Development or Operations create Teaching Demand?
* Who owns demand priority when capacity is insufficient?
* How are tentative forecasts distinguished from confirmed demand?

### Teaching Assignment

* What information constitutes an authorized Teaching Assignment?
* Who proposes, approves, acknowledges, changes, and cancels it?
* Which assignment roles are required?
* Can an assignment apply to one student, multiple groups, or only a learning unit?
* How are co-teaching, shared responsibility, assessors, moderators, and supervisors represented?
* May one assignment cover multiple campuses or delivery modes?
* What does assignment completion mean?

### Workload

* ~~Which activities contribute to teacher workload?~~ **Resolved.** Teacher workload is calculated from approved Teaching Assignments.
* ~~What unit and calculation rules apply?~~ **Resolved.** Workload Unit is configurable school policy; default unit is Teaching Hours. Phase-2 workload management focuses on assignment capacity, workload visibility, and assignment planning. Phase-2 does not introduce payroll calculations, compensation calculations, performance evaluation, or HR management functions.
* Are preparation, assessment, advising, supervision, and non-contact responsibilities included? *(School configuration — governed by Workload Unit policy. Not a blocking policy question.)*
* ~~Which limits are warnings and which prohibit assignment?~~ **Resolved.** Capacity limits support two categories: Warning (Approaching Limit — assignment remains permitted); Prohibited (Capacity Exceeded — assignment not permitted without approved exception). Capacity thresholds are policy-driven.
* ~~Who may approve overload or other exceptions?~~ **Resolved.** Workload exceptions require Teaching Authority approval. Workflow: Teaching Coordinator recommends → Teaching Authority approves → Workload Exception Granted. Teachers may not self-approve workload exceptions. Exception decisions must be auditable.
* How is planned workload reconciled with completed academic work? *(School configuration — governed by workload policy version and period. Not a blocking policy question.)*
* Does under-allocation require action or merely reporting? *(School configuration — not a blocking policy question.)*

### Scheduling Boundary

* What information must Teaching provide before Operations can schedule work?
* Can Operations schedule tentative work before assignment approval?
* How are timetable conflicts returned to Teaching?
* Does a schedule cancellation affect the Teaching Assignment automatically?
* Who decides between schedule change, substitute, and reassignment?

### Substitution and Reassignment

* Who may request and approve substitution?
* Which qualification and authorization rules apply to short-term coverage?
* May emergency coverage occur before formal approval, and how is it regularized?
* How is partial coverage represented?
* What handover is required?
* How are past academic records attributed after reassignment?

### Homeroom and Advising

* Is homeroom placement owned by Students, Teaching, or Operations?
* Is homeroom responsibility a Teaching Assignment?
* Is academic advising an Academic responsibility, Teaching Assignment role, or both?
* How is student-group membership separated from teacher responsibility for that group?

### Development and Extracurricular Boundary

* When is extracurricular facilitation a Teaching Assignment?
* Who owns facilitator capability, availability, and workload?
* Does Development request capacity or directly appoint a facilitator?
* Which student-support responsibilities may be assigned through Teaching?
* What minimum Development information may be disclosed to an assigned teacher?

### Record Lifecycle and Access

* Which Teaching records may teachers view or challenge?
* Which qualification evidence requires restricted access?
* What retention periods apply?
* Which records may be corrected, sealed, anonymized, or lawfully deleted?
* How are late corrections handled after academic results or schedules have been finalized?
* What happens to open assignments when a teacher leaves the school?

## Implementation Guardrails

Before implementing a Teaching feature:

1. Identify the real teaching-resource workflow and actor.
2. Identify the work demand and the domain that owns its purpose and outcome.
3. Distinguish Teaching Profile, capability, qualification evidence, verification, authorization, eligibility, availability, assignment, and schedule.
4. Identify the assignment role, scope, authority, effective dates, and workload commitment.
5. Identify the applicable qualification, authorization, availability, and workload policy versions.
6. Identify information owned by Students, Academics, Development, Finance, Operations, or a future employee capability.
7. Identify what Teaching owns and the minimum output another domain needs.
8. Preserve demand, evaluation, approval, assignment, substitution, exception, completion, and correction history.
9. Determine whether acknowledgement, approval, exception, handover, notification, or restricted access is required.
10. Implement the minimum workflow.

Do not begin from:

* database tables
* a teacher CRUD screen
* a timetable grid
* a workload dashboard
* employee or payroll records
* REST endpoints
* a class-teacher foreign key
* an LMS role
* a generic workforce-management product

Begin from the authorized teaching-resource workflow.

Do not invent:

* teacher categories or roles
* capability taxonomy
* required qualifications or equivalencies
* verification criteria
* teaching-authorization authority
* eligibility rules
* availability approval
* workload units, weights, limits, or formulas
* overload consequences or exception authority
* assignment priority
* acceptance or acknowledgement requirements
* substitution or emergency-coverage rules
* homeroom ownership
* employee status or contractual meaning
* performance appraisal meaning
* retention periods or access rights

When policy or workflow is unclear, record it as an open question and stop before encoding the assumption as a business rule.

For every proposed concept, ask:

```text
Does this describe a teaching resource, teaching capability,
qualification, authorization, availability, assignment,
workload commitment, substitution, or teaching history?

If not, which domain owns it?

What work requires a teaching resource, and which domain owns that work?

Is this a claimed capability, verified qualification,
school authorization, eligibility result, or approved assignment?

Does it describe potential availability, authorized responsibility,
an operational schedule, or work actually performed?

Which actor has authority, which dates apply,
and is this required by a real workflow now?
```

## Summary

The Teaching domain answers these primary business questions:

```text
Who is recognized as a teaching resource?

What teaching capabilities and verified qualifications does the person have?

What teaching work is the person authorized to perform?

When and under what conditions is the person available?

What teaching work requires staffing?

Who is eligible and has capacity for that work?

Who has been assigned, in what role, and for what effective period?

Does the assignment create a capacity or authorization conflict?

How is an absence, substitution, reassignment, or workload exception handled?

What teaching responsibilities and authorization decisions must be preserved historically?
```

It does not answer:

```text
Who is the student or what is the student's official status?

What must the student learn, what academic work occurred,
or what result did the student achieve?

What behaviour, counselling, wellbeing, or extracurricular outcome applies?

When and where is the activity operationally scheduled?

What does the student owe, or what payment was made?

What is the teacher's employment contract, salary, payroll,
general personnel record, or formal employee appraisal?
```

Those questions belong to Students, Academics, Development, Operations, Finance, and a future employee or Human Resources capability respectively.
