# Operations Domain

## Purpose

The Operations domain manages the shared time, place, event, communication, and operational coordination context required for school work to occur.

It is the authoritative source for:

* the approved academic and school calendar
* operational periods, school days, holidays, and closures
* the operational timetable
* when and where an approved activity is scheduled
* room availability, reservation, and allocation
* the operational lifecycle of school events
* operational announcements and their publication status
* operational feasibility decisions and exceptions
* schedule, room, and event conflicts and their resolution
* the history of approved operational arrangements and changes

The Operations domain begins when the school establishes shared operating context or receives a request requiring operational coordination. It continues through planning, feasibility review, approval, scheduling, publication, change, cancellation, completion, and required historical preservation.

Operations determines when, where, and under what operational authorization school activities occur.

It does not determine student status, learning requirements or results, student-development outcomes, financial outcomes, or who is authorized and assigned to teach.

---

## Domain Responsibility

The Operations domain owns:

* academic year and operational period context
* school calendar
* school day, holiday, and closure designation
* operational activity request
* operational feasibility review
* operational timetable and scheduled occurrence
* room and location context required for allocation
* room availability, reservation, and allocation
* operational conflict and resolution
* school event operational lifecycle
* announcement publication and withdrawal
* operational approval, condition, rejection, and exception
* postponement, relocation, rescheduling, and cancellation
* operational coordination status
* Operations history and correction

Operations is the authoritative source for approved shared calendar, timetable, room allocation, school-event context, announcement publication, and operational coordination status.

Other domains own the purpose, requirements, participants, decisions, and outcomes of their work. They provide the minimum information Operations needs to coordinate it. They must not independently publish a conflicting authoritative timetable, room allocation, or school calendar.

Operations provides operational context. It must not absorb another domain's business process merely because that process requires a date, room, announcement, or approval.

## Scope

### In Scope

#### Academic Year and Operational Period

Manage the shared time periods used to coordinate school work.

This may include:

* academic year
* term, semester, quarter, or another approved period
* admission or enrollment window
* assessment window
* reporting or transition period
* event period
* campus-specific operating period
* effective dates
* approval and version history

Operations owns when an approved period begins and ends. The requesting domain owns what its work means within that period.

Period names and structures are school-policy questions. Operations must not assume every school uses terms, semesters, grade levels, or a single campus calendar.

#### School Calendar

Manage the approved calendar that supplies common date context to all domains.

Calendar information may include:

* instructional and non-instructional days
* holidays
* school closures
* staff-only or student-facing days when required for coordination
* significant school dates
* domain-requested windows
* campus or program applicability
* publication status
* approved amendments

A calendar date does not by itself create admission, learning, Development, Finance, or Teaching work.

The owning domain must request or establish the work to which the date applies.

#### Operational Activity Request

Receive a request to place domain-owned work into operational time and space.

A request is actionable only when it contains the minimum required information:

* Source Owner (requesting domain and responsible actor)
* Purpose (source activity reference)
* Requested Time or Period (requested period, date, or recurrence)
* Responsible Actor
* Operational Requirements or Constraints

Additional information a request may include:

* expected duration
* campus, delivery mode, room, or capacity requirement
* participant or audience context
* accessibility, safety, confidentiality, or supervision constraint
* assigned teacher or facilitator context when required
* priority and flexibility supplied under approved policy
* dependencies and required clearances

Requests missing required information must be returned for clarification. Operations must not infer missing business intent. Feasibility review begins only after the request is actionable.

The request is not the underlying activity and does not transfer ownership of it to Operations.

Source approval from the owning domain is mandatory before scheduling may proceed. Tentative scheduling is permitted before Teaching Assignment finalization when the activity is approved but the teacher is not yet finalized; final allocation requires approved Teaching Assignments.

#### Operational Feasibility

Determine whether a proposed operational arrangement can be supported.

Feasibility may consider:

* calendar compatibility
* room availability and suitability
* capacity
* authorized teaching availability or assignment supplied by Teaching
* conflicts with approved activities
* dependency completion
* campus or delivery constraints
* permitted support, restriction, or confidentiality context
* required operational authority

Feasible, infeasible, conditionally feasible, and unresolved are distinct meanings.

Operational feasibility does not determine academic eligibility, student status, disciplinary outcome, financial standing, or teacher eligibility.

#### Operational Timetable and Scheduled Occurrence

Manage the approved placement of work into time and, where applicable, location.

A Scheduled Occurrence may identify:

* source activity
* date and time
* duration
* recurrence
* campus and room
* delivery mode
* applicable participants or audience reference
* assigned teaching-resource reference when needed
* approved status
* conditions and exceptions
* publication status
* change or cancellation history

A timetable is an operational arrangement. It is not a learning structure, Student Learning Plan, Teaching Assignment, Development program, or student-status record.

#### Room and Location Context

Manage the minimum location information required for operational allocation.

Phase-2 uses a simplified room model. Required room attributes:

* Room Type: Classroom, Laboratory, Hall, Meeting Room, Other
* Capacity: Maximum Occupancy
* Suitability characteristics (examples): Projector Available, Computer Available, Laboratory Equipment Available, Accessible

Room allocation decisions use only room type, capacity, and suitability.

Additional information a room context record may include:

* campus or site reference
* active or unavailable status
* effective dates
* permitted use constraints

Operations may maintain the operational identity of rooms when no separate facilities capability exists.

It does not thereby own building management, asset management, safety compliance, maintenance management, inventory, procurement, security systems, or facility engineering. Those boundaries are explicitly out of scope for Phase-2.

#### Room Availability, Reservation, and Allocation

Manage when a room may be used and which approved occurrence may use it.

This may include:

* availability window
* temporary restriction or closure
* reservation request
* tentative hold
* approved allocation
* allocation condition
* conflict
* relocation
* release or cancellation

**Tentative Holds** are supported. A Tentative Hold allows temporary reservation while final approval or allocation is pending. Default expiry: 48 hours unless converted into an approved reservation or allocation. Expired holds are automatically released. Schools may configure hold duration if required. Tentative Holds do not guarantee final allocation.

**Conflict classification:**

Prohibited conflicts (block allocation approval):
* Double Booking
* Room Unavailable
* Capacity Exceeded

Warning conflicts (inform decision-making; do not block allocation):
* Preferred Room Unavailable
* Near Capacity Threshold
* Alternative Room Recommended
* Scheduling Preference Conflict

**Room allocation overrides** require Operations Authority approval. Overrides must record: reason, approving authority, and affected allocations. Historical allocation records must remain traceable.

**Priority order** when resources are insufficient:
1. Academic Activities
2. Assessment Activities
3. Student Administrative Activities
4. School Events
5. Other Activities

Tie-breaker within the same priority level: First Approved Request Wins (determined by approval timestamp). No scoring engine, optimization engine, or weighted prioritization model is required in Phase-2.

A request, tentative hold, reservation, and approved allocation are different business meanings.

Room allocation must not silently create the underlying activity or approve another domain's decision.

#### School Event

Manage the operational lifecycle of a school event.

This may include:

* event proposal
* event purpose owner
* organizer
* audience
* date, time, location, and capacity
* dependencies and operational requirements
* feasibility decision
* approval and publication
* postponement, relocation, cancellation, and completion

Operations owns the event as an operational occurrence.

Academics owns academic purpose and results. Development owns extracurricular purpose, participation, and development outcomes. Students owns administrative transitions. Finance owns charges, payments, refunds, and financial clearance. Teaching owns teaching assignments.

#### Operational Announcement

Manage authorized publication of information needed for school coordination.

An announcement may include:

* source and requesting domain
* authorized publisher
* approved content or content reference
* audience
* channel
* publication and expiry time
* acknowledgement requirement when policy requires it
* correction, replacement, or withdrawal
* delivery or acknowledgement status when required

Operations owns publication status, not the underlying business fact.

An announcement must not become an alternative student decision, academic result, Development case record, financial statement, or Teaching Assignment.

#### Operational Approval and Exception

Manage decisions concerning operational feasibility and coordination.

These decisions may concern:

* time and location
* room suitability and capacity
* schedule or calendar exception
* event readiness
* publication
* conflict resolution
* postponement, relocation, or cancellation
* emergency operational action

Operations may record approval steps for cross-domain coordination, but the business decision remains with its owning domain.

Approval, acknowledgement, recommendation, clearance, feasibility, and execution must remain distinguishable.

#### Conflict and Disruption Management

Manage operational conflicts and disruptions affecting approved or proposed arrangements.

Examples include:

* competing room requests
* overlapping participant or teacher commitments
* room unavailability
* school or campus closure
* capacity or suitability conflict
* unavailable assigned teacher reported by Teaching
* dependency not satisfied
* event postponement
* emergency schedule change

Operations may reschedule, relocate, condition, reject, or escalate according to approved authority.

It must not independently reassign a teacher, change academic evidence, change a student's status, determine a Development outcome, or waive a financial obligation.

#### Operations History and Correction

Preserve approved calendars, schedules, allocations, events, announcements, decisions, changes, cancellations, and corrections.

History should distinguish:

* what was proposed
* what was approved
* what was published
* what was changed
* when the change became effective
* who authorized it
* why it changed
* which parties were notified

Correction of an inaccurate record is distinct from a new operational decision caused by changed circumstances.

## Out of Scope

### Students

Operations does not own:

* applicant or student identity
* parent or guardian relationship
* admission decision
* enrollment
* administrative placement
* student status
* leave, transfer, withdrawal, promotion, graduation, or alumni status

### Academics

Operations does not own:

* learning structure or requirement
* Student Learning Plan
* learning activity purpose or content
* academic attendance
* assignment or assessment
* academic result, progress, standing, or eligibility
* academic intervention

### Development

Operations does not own:

* behaviour or discipline
* counselling, wellbeing, or safeguarding case
* Development referral or intervention
* extracurricular purpose, participation, or outcome
* non-academic achievement

### Finance

Operations does not own:

* financial policy or obligation
* billing or receivable
* scholarship, discount, or waiver
* payment, allocation, credit, or refund
* financial clearance decision

### Teaching

Operations does not own:

* Teaching Profile
* capability, qualification, or authorization
* teacher availability as a source decision
* Teaching Demand
* Teaching Assignment
* teacher workload, substitution, or reassignment

Operations consumes authorized assignment and availability context to schedule work.

### Other Exclusions

* employee and Human Resources administration
* payroll and compensation
* accounting
* inventory and procurement
* facility maintenance and property management
* library
* transportation
* hostel
* cafeteria
* physical security operations
* clinical or emergency-response management
* generic identity and access management
* generic workflow or approval-platform ownership
* email, SMS, or notification infrastructure implementation

These capabilities may provide operational constraints or execute communications. They do not become part of Operations without explicit business justification.

## Actors

Actor names and separation of duties may differ by school.

### Operations Coordinator

Coordinates shared operational work.

May:

* receive requests
* identify dependencies and conflicts
* coordinate resolution
* monitor readiness
* escalate unresolved work

### Calendar Authority

Approves the academic year, common calendar, closures, and calendar amendments within policy.

### Scheduler or Timetable Officer

May:

* review scheduling requests
* create proposed arrangements
* allocate time and room
* detect conflicts
* publish approved schedules
* coordinate changes and cancellations

The scheduler cannot create a Teaching Assignment or redefine source-domain requirements.

### Room or Location Coordinator

Maintains operational room context, restrictions, reservations, and allocations.

### Event Organizer

Requests and coordinates an event. The organizer may belong to any domain.

The organizer does not automatically possess approval or publication authority.

### Communications Officer

Publishes, corrects, replaces, or withdraws authorized announcements.

### Operations Authority or School Leadership

May approve calendars, material events, exceptions, closures, conflict resolutions, and corrections according to policy.

Leadership participation does not transfer ownership of Students, Academics, Development, Finance, or Teaching decisions to Operations.

### Requesting-Domain Actor

Supplies the purpose, requirements, authority, and minimum context for work requiring coordination. The requesting domain remains responsible for its business decision and outcome.

### Student, Parent, Guardian, Teacher, or Recipient

May receive schedules, event information, changes, cancellations, or announcements according to purpose and authority.

Receipt does not imply permission to view all operational or source-domain information.

## Core Business Concepts

### Academic Year

An approved common period used to organize school operations.

### Operational Period

A bounded period with an approved coordination purpose.

### School Calendar

The approved set of dates and designations supplying shared operating context.

### Operational Activity Request

A request to coordinate domain-owned work in time, place, or shared operations.

### Scheduled Occurrence

An approved operational placement of source-owned work into time and location.

### Room

An operationally identifiable location that may be allocated.

### Room Availability

The time and conditions under which a room may be considered for allocation.

### Room Allocation

The approved use of a room by a Scheduled Occurrence.

### School Event

A coordinated school occurrence with an operational lifecycle and a clearly identified purpose owner.

### Operational Announcement

An authorized publication for a defined audience, purpose, channel, and period.

### Operational Feasibility Decision

A determination that a proposed arrangement is feasible, conditionally feasible, infeasible, or unresolved.

### Operational Conflict

An incompatibility between requests, constraints, or approved arrangements requiring action.

### Operational Exception

An authorized departure from an approved operational rule or arrangement.

### Operations Record Correction

An authorized correction that preserves the original record and reason for change.

## Operational Coordination Journey

The conceptual journey is:

```text
Common operating context established
        ↓
Domain-owned work requests coordination
        ↓
Requirements and authority clarified
        ↓
Operational feasibility reviewed
        ↓
Time and room proposed
        ↓
Conflict resolved or exception authorized
        ↓
Arrangement approved and published
        ↓
Change, disruption, or cancellation coordinated
        ↓
Occurrence completed or closed
        ↓
History and corrections preserved
```

Not every request must pass through every step. The minimum workflow depends on risk, policy, scale, and authority.

## Main Workflows

## 1. Establish or Amend Academic Year and School Calendar

### Trigger

A new operating cycle is planned, or an approved calendar requires change.

### Actor

* Calendar Authority
* Operations Coordinator
* School Leadership
* authorized domain representatives

### Input

* proposed period and dates
* campus or program applicability
* legally or institutionally required dates
* requested windows from owning domains
* existing approved calendar
* approval authority
* reason and effective date for amendment

### Outcome

* calendar is proposed, approved, rejected, amended, or superseded
* affected domains receive authoritative context
* history and publication status are preserved

### Business Rules

* an academic year and its periods must have explicit effective dates
* approval and publication are distinct
* amendment must not silently rewrite the previously approved calendar
* a calendar date must not create another domain's decision
* retroactive amendment requires explicit authority and impact review

## 2. Submit and Clarify Operational Activity Request

### Trigger

Domain-owned work requires time, location, publication, or shared coordination.

### Actor

* Requesting-Domain Actor
* Operations Coordinator
* Scheduler

### Input

* source activity and owner
* requested arrangement
* requirements and constraints
* responsible actor
* required assignment, approval, or clearance context

### Outcome

* request is actionable, returned for clarification, withdrawn, or rejected as outside scope
* ownership and missing dependencies are explicit

### Business Rules

* every request must identify its source owner and purpose
* Operations must request only the minimum necessary information
* an incomplete request must not be treated as approved
* request submission does not reserve time or room unless policy explicitly defines a hold

## 3. Review Operational Feasibility

### Trigger

An actionable request is ready for operational review.

### Actor

* Operations Coordinator
* Scheduler
* Room Coordinator
* Operations Authority when required

### Input

* request
* calendar and existing timetable
* room availability and requirements
* approved teacher assignment and availability context when applicable
* permitted domain constraints
* applicable policy

### Outcome

* request is feasible, conditionally feasible, infeasible, or unresolved
* conflicts, conditions, and required decisions are explicit

### Business Rules

* feasibility must be explainable from current authorized context
* Operations must not infer another domain's approval from operational feasibility
* unresolved information must not be treated as confirmation
* conditions must identify who owns their satisfaction

## 4. Schedule an Activity and Allocate a Room

### Trigger

A feasible request has sufficient authority and dependencies.

### Actor

* Scheduler
* Room Coordinator
* Operations Authority when required

### Input

* approved or authorized source activity
* feasible time options
* duration and recurrence
* room and capacity requirements
* assignment and availability context
* conflicts, priority, and conditions

### Outcome

* Scheduled Occurrence and room allocation are proposed or approved
* requesting domain receives the arrangement
* publication status is explicit

### Business Rules

* time and room allocation must use information effective for the occurrence
* tentative and approved arrangements must be distinguishable
* one allocation must not create an unauthorized double booking
* scheduling must not create or change a Teaching Assignment
* recurring schedules must preserve individual exceptions and changes

## 5. Resolve Operational Conflict or Exception

### Trigger

Requests or constraints cannot coexist, or an approved rule requires exception consideration.

### Actor

* Operations Coordinator
* Scheduler
* affected Requesting-Domain Actors
* Operations Authority

### Input

* conflicting requests or arrangements
* constraint and impact
* applicable priority and exception policy
* alternatives
* authority

### Outcome

* conflict is resolved, exception approved or rejected, or matter escalated
* affected arrangements and notifications are explicit

### Business Rules

* priority and override authority must come from policy
* automated conflict detection is evidence, not an unexplained decision
* another domain must decide changes to concepts it owns
* exception must record scope, reason, authority, and effective period

## 6. Plan and Approve School Event

### Trigger

A proposed school event requires operational coordination.

### Actor

* Event Organizer
* Operations Coordinator
* Scheduler or Room Coordinator
* Operations Authority

### Input

* purpose owner and organizer
* intended audience
* proposed time and place
* capacity and operational requirements
* dependencies and clearances
* communication needs

### Outcome

* event is proposed, approved, conditionally approved, rejected, or returned for revision
* operational arrangement and publication authority are explicit

### Business Rules

* every event must have an identified purpose owner
* operational approval does not approve academic, Development, Students, Finance, or Teaching outcomes
* event and Scheduled Occurrence may be related but must retain their meanings
* approval, publication, and occurrence are distinct

## 7. Publish or Withdraw Operational Announcement

### Trigger

Authorized information must be communicated, corrected, replaced, or withdrawn.

### Actor

* Communications Officer
* requesting-domain authority
* Operations Authority when required

### Input

* approved content or source reference
* purpose and audience
* publication channel and timing
* expiry or acknowledgement requirement
* authority

### Outcome

* announcement is scheduled, published, corrected, replaced, expired, or withdrawn
* delivery or acknowledgement status is available when required

### Business Rules

* source-domain truth must not be rewritten by the publication workflow
* audience and disclosure authority must be explicit
* correction must remain distinguishable from withdrawal or replacement
* failed delivery is not the same as recipient acknowledgement

## 8. Record Room Restriction or Unavailability

### Trigger

A room cannot be used or its permitted use changes.

### Actor

* Room Coordinator
* authorized external facilities or safety actor
* Operations Authority

### Input

* room
* restriction and source
* effective period
* reason
* affected allocations

### Outcome

* room availability is changed
* affected arrangements are identified for review
* relocation or cancellation work is initiated when required

### Business Rules

* external notice is evidence for the Operations workflow
* unavailability must not silently cancel affected occurrences
* Operations records operational effect without inventing maintenance or compliance findings

## 9. Reschedule, Relocate, Postpone, or Cancel

### Trigger

An approved occurrence or event cannot proceed as arranged, or an authorized change is requested.

### Actor

* Event Organizer or Requesting-Domain Actor
* Scheduler
* Operations Coordinator
* Operations Authority when required

### Input

* current approved arrangement
* reason and source
* affected participants and dependencies
* alternatives
* required authorization

### Outcome

* arrangement is rescheduled, relocated, postponed, cancelled, or retained
* affected domains and audiences receive authorized change information
* previous arrangement remains historically interpretable

### Business Rules

* change effective time and publication time must remain distinguishable
* cancellation of a schedule does not automatically cancel its source activity or Teaching Assignment
* downstream impact must be identified
* retroactive change must not falsify what actually occurred

## 10. Coordinate School or Campus Closure

### Trigger

An authorized decision or urgent condition affects school operation.

### Actor

* Calendar Authority
* Operations Authority or School Leadership
* Operations Coordinator
* Communications Officer

### Input

* closure authority and reason
* affected campus, population, and period
* current calendar, timetable, events, and allocations
* communication requirements

### Outcome

* closure is approved and recorded
* affected arrangements are identified
* authorized notifications are published
* recovery, rescheduling, or domain-owned follow-up is initiated

### Business Rules

* emergency authority and later confirmation must follow policy
* closure does not independently determine attendance, fees, learning completion, or employee consequences
* scope and effective dates must be explicit

## 11. Close Completed Operational Arrangement

### Trigger

An occurrence or event has ended or its operational work is complete.

### Actor

* Operations Coordinator
* Scheduler
* Event Organizer

### Input

* approved arrangement
* occurrence or cancellation status
* unresolved operational issues
* required source-domain confirmation

### Outcome

* arrangement is completed, cancelled, or remains open for follow-up
* operational history is preserved

### Business Rules

* operational completion is not academic completion, Development outcome, financial settlement, or Teaching Assignment completion
* Operations must not fabricate attendance or participation to close work

## 12. Correct Approved Operations Record

### Trigger

An approved Operations record is inaccurate or incomplete.

### Actor

* authorized Operations actor
* Operations Authority
* affected source-domain actor when required

### Input

* original record
* evidence of error
* corrected information
* reason, authority, and effective meaning
* downstream impact

### Outcome

* correction is approved or rejected
* original and corrected meanings remain traceable
* affected consumers receive correction information

### Business Rules

* approved records must never be silently overwritten
* correction is distinct from a later operational change
* correction must not rewrite another domain's record
* retroactive correction requires impact review

## Business Rules

### Context, Not Ownership Transfer

Operations coordinates work owned by other domains. A date, room, event, announcement, or approval step must not transfer ownership of the underlying business decision.

### Purpose Before Scheduling

Every scheduled occurrence must reference a legitimate source purpose and owner. Operations must not create unexplained timetable entries merely to fill a grid.

### Activity, Assignment, and Schedule Are Distinct

Academics or Development owns the activity purpose. Teaching owns who is authorized and assigned. Operations owns when and where it is scheduled.

### Request, Hold, Allocation, and Occurrence Are Distinct

A requested room is not reserved. A tentative hold is not approval. An approved allocation does not prove that the event occurred.

### Approval Meanings Are Explicit

Operational feasibility, operational approval, source-domain approval, financial clearance, acknowledgement, and publication are different decisions.

### Conflicts Require Policy and Authority

Do not invent priority, automatically override an approved arrangement, or treat first submission as universal precedence.

### Effective Dating

Requested date, approval date, publication date, effective date, occurrence date, change date, cancellation date, and recording date may differ. Their meanings must remain distinguishable.

### Historical Integrity

Preserve approved calendars, schedules, allocations, events, announcements, exceptions, cancellations, and corrections. Do not recalculate history under newer policy without authorized retroactive action.

### No Automatic Cross-Domain Consequence

A closure, conflict, cancellation, missed occurrence, or lack of operational approval must not automatically change:

* student status
* academic attendance, evidence, result, or standing
* Development outcome
* financial obligation, payment, or clearance
* Teaching Assignment or workload

The owning domain determines its consequence under approved policy.

### Minimum Necessary Information

Operations consumes only information required to coordinate the authorized work. It must not copy full student, counselling, financial, academic, or personnel records for convenience.

### No Silent Deletion

Expiry, withdrawal, cancellation, supersession, correction, or lawful deletion must not silently erase required operational history.

## Reporting Responsibility

Operations may provide operational information such as:

* calendars awaiting approval or publication
* operational requests awaiting clarification or review
* proposed, approved, and published scheduled occurrences
* room availability, utilization, restriction, and conflict
* timetable conflicts awaiting resolution
* events awaiting readiness, approval, publication, or follow-up
* announcements awaiting publication, expiry, correction, or withdrawal
* exceptions and conditional approvals requiring action
* changes, relocations, postponements, and cancellations
* affected audiences awaiting notification
* completed arrangements awaiting closure
* Operations corrections requiring downstream communication

Reports must originate from real Operations workflows.

Do not create separate data-entry processes solely for dashboards.

Cross-domain dashboards may combine Operations information with Students, Academics, Development, Finance, and Teaching information, but ownership, effective date, publication status, confidentiality, and access authority must remain explicit.

Room utilization is an operational measure. It must not be presented as academic quality, teacher performance, student participation, or financial efficiency without an explicitly owned analysis.

## Guiding Principles

### Coordination First

Design around how authorized school work is made operationally possible and understandable.

### Time, Place, and Operational Authority

Operations owns shared coordination context: when, where, and under what operational arrangement work may occur.

### Source Purpose Remains Source-Owned

Scheduling or announcing work does not transfer its purpose, participants, evidence, decisions, or outcomes to Operations.

### One Authoritative Operational Arrangement

Avoid independent conflicting calendars, timetables, event schedules, and room allocations.

### Explicit State and Meaning

Proposed, tentative, feasible, approved, published, changed, cancelled, completed, and corrected are distinct meanings.

### Preserve Operational History

Keep past arrangements interpretable, including what audiences were told at the time.

### Privacy by Purpose

Scheduling visibility and announcement audiences depend on purpose and authority. Operational convenience does not justify unrestricted disclosure.

### Actor-Centric Workspaces

Show each actor what request, conflict, condition, approval, publication, or change needs attention and what action they may take.

### Simple Before Flexible

Implement the smallest calendar, scheduling, room, event, announcement, conflict, and approval workflows required by the current school. Do not create a speculative universal workflow, facilities, or enterprise-resource system.

## Open Questions

### MVP Scope

* Which calendar, scheduling, room, event, and announcement workflows are required first?
* Is a full timetable required, or only event and room coordination?
* Which actors need operational work queues?
* Which information must be published to students, parents, and teachers?

### Calendar

* Which period types exist, and who approves them?
* Is there one calendar or separate campus and program calendars?
* Which dates may owning domains request or control?
* Who may authorize prospective, urgent, or retroactive amendments?
* How are closures and replacement days handled?

### Scheduling

* ~~What constitutes an actionable scheduling request?~~ **Resolved.** An Operational Activity Request is actionable only when it contains: Source Owner, Purpose, Requested Time or Period, Responsible Actor, and Operational Requirements or Constraints. Incomplete requests must be returned for clarification; Operations must not infer missing business intent; feasibility review begins only after the request is actionable.
* ~~May tentative work be scheduled before source approval or Teaching Assignment approval?~~ **Resolved.** Source activity approval from the owning domain is mandatory before scheduling may proceed. Tentative scheduling is permitted before Teaching Assignment finalization when the activity is approved but the teacher is not yet finalized. Final allocation requires approved Teaching Assignments.
* Which recurrence and exception patterns are required? *(School configuration — not a blocking policy question.)*
* ~~Who owns scheduling priority when demands compete?~~ **Resolved.** Priority order: 1. Academic Activities; 2. Assessment Activities; 3. Student Administrative Activities; 4. School Events; 5. Other Activities. Tie-breaker within the same priority level: First Approved Request Wins (by approval timestamp). No scoring engine, optimization engine, or weighted prioritization model is required in Phase-2.
* ~~Which conflicts prohibit approval and which are warnings?~~ **Resolved.** Prohibited conflicts (block approval): Double Booking, Room Unavailable, Capacity Exceeded. Warning conflicts (inform but do not block): Preferred Room Unavailable, Near Capacity Threshold, Alternative Room Recommended, Scheduling Preference Conflict.
* Does cancellation affect the source activity automatically? *(Not a blocking policy question; each source domain owns cancellation of its own activity.)*

### Rooms and Locations

* Does Operations own campus and room definitions or only allocation? *(Operations maintains the minimum room context required for allocation when no separate facilities capability exists. Not a blocking policy question.)*
* ~~Which room suitability and capacity characteristics are required?~~ **Resolved.** Phase-2 uses a simplified room model. Room Type: Classroom, Laboratory, Hall, Meeting Room, Other. Capacity: Maximum Occupancy. Suitability characteristics (examples): Projector Available, Computer Available, Laboratory Equipment Available, Accessible. Building management, asset management, safety compliance, maintenance management, and facility engineering remain out of scope.
* ~~Are tentative holds permitted, and when do they expire?~~ **Resolved.** Tentative Holds are supported. Default expiry: 48 hours unless converted into an approved reservation or allocation. Expired holds are automatically released; schools may configure hold duration if required.
* Are non-room resources schedulable even though inventory is out of scope? *(Out of scope for Phase-2 — not a blocking policy question.)*
* ~~Who may restrict, override, relocate, or release a room?~~ **Resolved.** Room allocation overrides require Operations Authority approval. Overrides must record: reason, approving authority, and affected allocations. Historical allocation records must remain traceable.
* Where is facility maintenance owned? *(Explicitly out of scope — not a blocking policy question.)*

### Events

* What distinguishes a School Event from a learning activity or extracurricular program?
* Which events require approval, financial clearance, or assigned teaching capacity?
* Who owns event attendance at general school events?
* What does event completion mean operationally?
* Which event changes require renewed approval?

### Announcements

* Which communication types belong to Operations?
* Who approves content, audience, and channel?
* Is delivery tracking required?
* When is acknowledgement required, and what proves it?
* How are urgent, corrected, translated, expired, or withdrawn announcements handled?

### Operational Approval and Exception

* Which decisions are genuinely operational approvals?
* Who may approve conditions, overrides, closures, and emergency action?
* Is Operations only coordinating an approval owned by another domain?
* How are temporary exceptions reviewed and expired?

### Domain Boundaries

* Is homeroom placement owned by Students, Teaching, or Operations?
* Does Operations own attendance at non-academic school events?
* When does an event remain an Operations event versus a Development program?
* What minimum sensitive context may Development share for scheduling?
* What information must Teaching provide before scheduling?
* What operational consequence follows a denied financial clearance, and who owns it?

### Record Lifecycle and Access

* Who may view tentative, restricted, or confidential arrangements?
* Which records may be corrected, sealed, anonymized, or lawfully deleted?
* What retention periods apply?
* How are late corrections handled after audiences relied on published information?
* Which external calendar or communication system, if any, is authoritative?

## Implementation Guardrails

Before implementing an Operations feature:

1. Identify the real coordination workflow, actor, and source-domain purpose.
2. Identify who owns the underlying activity, participant decision, assignment, financial result, and outcome.
3. Distinguish request, feasibility, approval, schedule, room allocation, publication, occurrence, completion, cancellation, and correction.
4. Identify the applicable calendar, time zone, campus, room, recurrence, and effective dates.
5. Identify constraints, conflicts, conditions, priority policy, and decision authority.
6. Identify information owned by Students, Academics, Development, Finance, Teaching, or an external capability.
7. Identify what Operations owns and the minimum output another domain or recipient needs.
8. Preserve proposals, approvals, publications, changes, cancellations, exceptions, notifications, and corrections.
9. Determine whether confidentiality, acknowledgement, separation of duties, or emergency authority applies.
10. Implement the minimum workflow.

Do not begin from:

* database tables
* a calendar widget
* a timetable grid
* a room CRUD screen
* an announcement composer
* REST endpoints
* a dashboard requirement
* a generic approval engine
* a generic booking product
* an enterprise resource-planning or facilities-management product

Begin from the authorized operational coordination workflow.

Do not invent:

* academic-year or period structure
* holidays, school days, or closure rules
* scheduling priority
* room types, capacity, or suitability rules
* tentative-hold behavior
* event categories or required approvals
* announcement audience or acknowledgement rules
* conflict-resolution authority
* operational exception criteria
* emergency authority
* cross-domain consequences
* attendance ownership
* homeroom ownership
* maintenance or inventory ownership
* retention periods or access rights

When policy or workflow is unclear, record it as an open question and stop before encoding the assumption as a business rule.

For every proposed concept, ask:

```text
Does this describe shared calendar, time, place, room allocation,
school event coordination, announcement publication,
operational feasibility, conflict, exception, or Operations history?

If not, which domain owns it?

What source-owned work is being coordinated, and who owns its purpose?

Is this a request, tentative hold, feasibility decision,
approval, allocation, publication, occurrence, or completion?

Does this decide when and where work occurs,
or does it improperly decide what another domain owns?

Which actor has authority, which dates and time zone apply,
who must be informed, and is this required by a real workflow now?
```

## Summary

The Operations domain answers these primary business questions:

```text
What academic year, period, school day, holiday, or closure applies?

What authorized work requires operational coordination?

Is the requested time and location operationally feasible?

When and where is the activity scheduled?

Which room has been reserved or allocated, and under what conditions?

What operational conflict or exception requires action?

Has the school event been approved, published, changed, or cancelled?

Which announcement was authorized for which audience and period?

Who must be informed of an operational change?

What approved operational arrangement and history must be preserved?
```

It does not answer:

```text
Who is the student or what is the student's official status?

What must the student learn, what academic activity occurred,
or what result did the student achieve?

What behaviour, counselling, wellbeing, extracurricular participation,
or Development outcome applies?

What does the student owe, what payment was made,
or whether financial clearance is granted?

Who is qualified, available, authorized, or assigned to teach,
and what workload is committed?

How are employees, payroll, accounting, inventory,
procurement, or facility maintenance managed?
```

Those questions belong to Students, Academics, Development, Finance, Teaching, or explicitly defined future capabilities respectively.
